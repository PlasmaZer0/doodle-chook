  • The bigger a black hole, the less tightly packed it has to be — Real physics: a mass M is a black hole once it fits inside a radius of 2GM/c², so a black hole's average density is 3c⁶/(32πG³M²), which falls with the square of its mass
  • At about 140 million Suns, a black hole is no denser than water — Real figure: that density equals 1,000 kg/m³ at M ≈ 1.36 × 10⁸ solar masses ≈ 2.7 × 10³⁸ kg (G = 6.674 × 10⁻¹¹, c = 2.998 × 10⁸ m/s, Sun = 1.989 × 10³⁰ kg). Its radius is about 4 × 10¹¹ m, 2.7 times the Earth's distance from the Sun
  • People are about as dense as water — Real figure: the human body averages about 985–1,010 kg/m³, which is why we float
  • An average adult weighs about 62 kg — Walpole et al. (2012), "The weight of nations: an estimation of adult human biomass", BMC Public Health 12:439
  • 2.7 × 10³⁸ kg ÷ 62 kg ≈ 4 × 10³⁶ people — Arithmetic: 4.35 × 10³⁶. A ball of that many people is inside its own event horizon with no squeezing. In practice it would collapse into a black hole even before it was complete
  • Inside a black hole, every future ends in the same place — Real physics: inside a non-rotating black hole's event horizon, every path into the future reaches the central singularity
  • 33 snaps for all of us, 122 for a black hole's worth — Arithmetic: each snap halves the crowd. 2³³ ≈ 8.6 × 10⁹ covers 8.2 billion people, and 2¹²² ≈ 5.3 × 10³⁶ covers 4.35 × 10³⁶
